## Mission

> **HumanOS** is an open-source computational framework for modeling and comparing human learning strategies under explicit assumptions.

HumanOS transforms discussions about learning, habits, and decision-making into **transparent, reproducible computational experiments**.

---

## The Core Shift

Most debates about learning strategies are qualitative:

> ❌ *"What is the best strategy?"*

HumanOS reframes the question:

> ✅ *"Under these explicit assumptions, which strategy performs better — and by how much?"*

This forces assumptions to be **stated**, **tested**, and **challenged** — not hidden.

---

## Framework Overview

```
Define Strategy
      │
      ▼
Specify Assumptions
(forgetting rate, attention span, prior knowledge, etc.)
      │
      ▼
Run Simulation
      │
      ├──► Strategy A performance over time
      └──► Strategy B performance over time
                    │
                    ▼
          Compare & Visualize
          (retention curves, efficiency metrics)
```

## Example Questions HumanOS Can Model

| Question | Strategies Compared |
|:---------|:-------------------|
| Massed vs. Spaced Practice | Cramming vs. Spaced Repetition |
| Passive vs. Active Recall | Re-reading vs. Flashcard Review |
| Depth vs. Breadth | Deep-dive one topic vs. survey many |
| Effect of prior knowledge | Novice vs. Expert learning curves |

---

## Why This Matters

Learning strategy research is full of contradictory advice. HumanOS takes a **first-principles approach**:

1. **Make assumptions explicit** — if a strategy depends on a specific forgetting rate, say so
2. **Simulate outcomes** — don't just argue theoretically
3. **Compare quantitatively** — measure, don't opine
4. **Open the model** — anyone can challenge your assumptions

## Philosophy

> *"A model is wrong, but some models are useful. HumanOS builds models that are at least honest about their assumptions."*

This project sits at the intersection of **cognitive science**, **decision theory**, and **computational simulation** — an unconventional but rigorous way to think about how humans learn.
